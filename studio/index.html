Screening AI-written slogans by sensory fit

In my experiment, Claude, GPT, and Gemini wrote slogans for 20 fictitious brands, all making the same brand promise. Each slogan has two scores: sensory fit, how similar its profile on 45 sensory rating scales is to the brand brief's, and message match, how similar its meaning is to the brief. Draw 48 of them and see which five the study's rule keeps.

What the study found. 153 people rated 3,672 of these AI-written slogans, eight per brand, mixed in random order. Slogans higher in sensory fit were rated more favorably: +.19 points on a 7-point scale per standard deviation of fit (95% CI .12 to .26), beyond message match, sentiment and word features. One draw of candidates is only an illustration of how the screening rule works; the evidence is the pooled data, shown for each brand below the candidates.

What people actually rated

The candidates are real lines that three AI models wrote live for participants in the study; each draw replays 48 of them, and the demo calls no model. Sensory fit is the cosine similarity of the slogan's and the brief's profiles (−1 to 1); message match is the cosine similarity of their text embeddings. The ratings chart shows the study's de-identified responses, each relative to the rater's own average; each slogan was rated by one person, and the per-brand breakdown is exploratory.
